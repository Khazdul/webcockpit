# Brief: "Sten och pixel" — pane frame + content mockups (round 2)

(If you read this file before it existed, just read it again now.)

## Context

WebCockpit is a MUD client for MUME (Middle-earth) that runs in the browser with a TUI look.
Panes (Group, Timers, …) sit on a cell grid. Today a pane has a thick grey "glass" rim with a
3D bevel and a nameplate hanging from the top rim, its title "engraved" (dark ink with a light lip).
The owner likes the retro game feel of that. Round 1 explored 5 styles for the *content* of the
Group and Timers panes; the owner picked **5 "Pixel"** (16-bit RPG windows, stepped corners,
hard 1px shadows, Pixelify Sans headers) as best, and found **4 "Runsten"** (carved stone, chamfered
corners, grainy texture, engraved text, knot ornaments) interesting. The CSS of both is in
`ref-4-runsten-5-pixel.css` (class scopes `.v4` / `.v5`) — read it.

The owner now says the current frame no longer fits: **the pane frame and its content must feel
like one object that melts together.** So this round redesigns BOTH the frame (border, nameplate,
title) AND the content, combining 4 and 5 in different ways, and exploring new ideas.

Owner's taste, in their words: softer, playful, depth, textures, ornaments instead of lines, a
retro game feel. Conversation with the owner is Swedish; UI content stays English.

## Hard rules

- **Do not change content or function**: no edits to base.html/JS/data; every element stays visible and readable
  (names, numbers, icons, colours per status/group, ending-soon red time, hatched NPC band bars, away row dimmed).
  The colour coding (`--c`) must still read clearly.
- Dark only (the Black preset; pane hole is near-black today, you may change the hole's fill/texture).
- **Frame must be implementable in the real app**: a pane's frame lives in its outer ring of grid cells (cell ≈ 9×17 px).
  Keep the visible border band ≈ 4–9 px on left/right/bottom; the top band incl. the nameplate/title must fit ≈ 17 px.
  Ornaments may sit in the corners within that ring. The frame must scale to any pane size (no fixed-size artwork
  spanning the whole frame; repeating patterns and corner pieces are fine). Two panes sit ~4 px apart.
- Content text stays 13 px mono (headers/labels may use Pixelify Sans or Cinzel). Readable contrast.
- Pixel-art looks: use crisp hard-stop gradients / box-shadow steps in whole px; they will be shown at 1.5× DPR.
- Animations optional, subtle; the base already disables them under prefers-reduced-motion.
- At narrow widths (≈360 px) nothing may overflow horizontally.
- Every selector scoped under your `.vNN`. Don't touch other agents' files.
- Write a short comment at the top of each CSS file: the concept and how the frame maps onto the cell ring.

## Directions (each agent gets two; make the two clearly different from each other)

- **01 Pixelsten** — stone texture but with pixel-stepped corners everywhere (frame and cards). The frame is a stone
  band with a 2-px pixel bevel (light top-left, dark bottom-right). Nameplate: a stepped stone tab with the title carved in pixel font.
- **02 Murverk** — dungeon-crawler wall: the frame band is tiny pixel bricks/blocks (repeating SVG pattern), the
  nameplate a keystone or carved lintel. Content cards are stone slabs set into the wall.
- **03 Huggen tavla** — the whole pane is ONE carved slab: no separate rim; the frame is the raised edge of the slab,
  the content area a carved recess in the same stone; cards are shallow raised areas of the same stone, so frame and
  content are literally one material. Pixel font only for headers/title.
- **04 Hörnstenar** — a slim JRPG window border (black + light line + black) with chunky stone corner blocks
  (use the .fx hooks) and a stone nameplate; content in pixel style with stone accents.
- **05 Runband** — the frame band carries a subtle repeating engraved rune/knot pattern in pixel style; knot ornaments
  in the corners; the title in a rune cartouche. Content echoes the pattern sparingly (e.g. header ornaments).
- **06 Mossig ruin** — playful: weathered stone frame with pixel moss/lichen tufts and cracks, warm torch light from
  above; content cards share the stone with small moss accents. Charming, not cluttered.
- **07 Obsidian** — polished dark volcanic stone with pixel gem studs (corners, nameplate); bars and chips as gem
  inlays whose colours echo the timer/status colours. Glossy but still pixel.
- **08 Fönster i berget** — a rough rock edge (irregular pixel outline) around a classic 16-bit RPG window panel;
  content in pure pixel-RPG style. The rock frames, the window holds.
- **09 Stilla pixel** — the calm one: thin stepped pixel border (1 px dark + 1 px light steps), muted stone tint,
  a small notched title tab; minimal ornament, maximal clarity. For long play sessions.
- **10 Fri idé** — invent something new that still combines stone and pixel and makes frame+content one object
  (e.g. 1-bit dither shading, isometric depth, ambient torch light, carved-in-pixel lettering, …). Surprise us,
  but keep it coherent and readable.
