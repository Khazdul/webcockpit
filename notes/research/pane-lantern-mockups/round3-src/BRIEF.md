# Brief: pane frame + content, round 3

## Context

WebCockpit is a MUD client for MUME (Middle-earth) that runs in the browser with a TUI look. Panes
(Group, Timers, …) sit on a cell grid. Today a pane has a thick grey "glass" rim with a slight 3D bevel
and a nameplate hanging from the top rim, its title "engraved" (dark ink, light lip). The owner likes the
depth and the slight retro game feel of that rim; the content inside (bars, chips) is flat and modern.

Round 1 explored content styles (`ref/round1.html`). Round 2 combined carved stone + 16-bit pixel and also
redesigned the frame. Round-2 favourites (CSS in `ref/`, scoped `.v07` `.v09` `.v10`):
- **07 Obsidian** — polished dark volcanic glass rim, 2-px bevels, stepped corners, diagonal gloss streaks,
  garnet studs in the corners and beside the title; bars as faceted gem inlays; chips dark glass with a
  border and fill in the group colour; each timer header line ends in a tiny gem in the group colour.
- **09 Stilla pixel** — calm: thin stepped pixel border (1 px black, 1 px light, 1 px dark), muted slate stone,
  a small notched title tab with stepped shoulders, quiet dark cards, dotted header lines; minimal ornament.
- **10 Fackelsken** — the pane as one block of stone shaded with ordered dither, the title carved in a light
  strip between two tiny flickering torches with warm light spilling down; cards/chips/bars as carved hollows.

**Owner feedback now (Swedish, paraphrased):** likes 7, 9 and 10 most. But all round-2 borders feel a bit
*too retro*; the retro touch should be dialled back somewhat in general. It must still be possible to
**choose a pane colour, switch the border off entirely, or make the pane background transparent — and it
must still look good** in all those cases. Earlier wishes still hold: the frame and its content should feel
like one object that melts together; softer, a bit playful, depth, textures and ornaments rather than lines.

So this round lands **somewhere between round 2 and today's look**. Picture: the depth and materials of
7/9/10, but with the restraint and finish of today's glass rim. Pixel-stepped details are seasoning now, not
the main course (a stepped corner or a pixel stud may stay; a whole pixel-art frame is too much).

## Markup (generated by base.html; read its JS)

```
.pair.vNN[data-tint][data-frame="on|off"][data-clear?][data-behind="black|weather"]  (the scene behind both panes)
  .pane (::before is today's rim, masked to the ring)  > i.fx.f1 … f4 (hidden hooks)
        > .hole > .plate > span.pt  (title) ; .hole > .gp (Group) | .tm (Timers)
  .gp : .fz (fight zone: .fs.tank/.fs.foe > .ft > .fn .fv ; .trk > .fill ; .vs)
        .m2 (member: .l1 > .mk .name(.pl) .tag .where .sts > .ic(.ring .glow .dimmed) .stime .vr .ic ; .bars > .bar(.band|.none) > .trk > .fill)
  .tm : .tg (--c) > .th (.hl svg span .cnt .hln) ; .items(.flow|.grid) > .chip(.soon .untimed .charm) > i.fill svg.ci .n .t .x
```
`--p` = percent on fills, `--c` = the element's colour (status/group/vital). Override base rules with your scoped selectors.

## Tokens and states you MUST handle (use the controls / query string to test every one)

- **Tints** (set on `.pair`): `--pane-fill`, `--pane-border`, `--pane-h`, `--pane-s`, `--pane-bg` (= fill, or
  transparent when clear), `--glass` (today's rim colour from the tint). The seven app tints: black(Plain), red,
  green, blue, grey, orange, purple. **Your frame material and content surfaces must follow the tint** (derive
  from these tokens with `color-mix`, `oklch(from …)`, hsl with --pane-h/--pane-s, etc.). Plain must look good
  too. Test at least Plain, one warm (red/orange) and one cool (blue/green) tint.
- **`[data-frame="off"]`**: no frame at all — no rim, no title/plate, no corner ornaments; the content alone
  must still look finished and belong to the same family (base already hides `.pane::before`, `.plate`, `.fx`
  and zeroes padding; you may refine, e.g. cards still carry the material). Your own frame pseudo-elements must
  be hidden too.
- **`[data-clear]`**: the pane background is transparent (things behind show through, sharp, no blur). The
  frame stays (in its tint, no blur), the hole shows what is behind, content must stay readable over the black
  page *and* over the weather sky (`[data-behind="weather"]`). Don't paint the rim under the content.
- **frame off + clear** together: content floats directly on the page/sky; must still read well.

## Hard rules

- **Do not change content or function**: no edits to base.html/JS/data; every element stays visible and
  readable (names, numbers, icons, status/group colours, ending-soon red time, hatched NPC band bars, dimmed away row).
- Dark only for now (the Black preset). Content text stays 13 px mono. Headers may use Pixelify Sans, Cinzel,
  Alegreya Sans or the mono; avoid Pixelify in ALL CAPS at small sizes (reads badly: "SANCTUARY" → "SANOTUARY").
- **Implementable**: the frame lives in the pane's outer ring of grid cells (cell ≈ 9×17 px): visible band ≈ 4–9 px
  left/right/bottom, top band incl. title ≤ ≈ 17 px; corner ornaments inside that ring; scales to any pane size
  (repeating patterns and corner pieces fine; no fixed artwork spanning the frame). Panes sit ~4 px apart.
  Textures via CSS gradients / inline `data:image/svg+xml` (feTurbulence ok; see `--stone-noise` in base), no images.
- Subtle animation only, if any. No horizontal overflow at ≈360 px viewport.
- Iterate: build → screenshot (default + the states above) → look at the PNGs with Read → refine. Aim 3–5 rounds.
- A short comment at the top of each CSS file: concept, how the frame maps onto the cell ring, how tint/off/clear are handled.

## Directions (you get two: one "between" (11–15) and one "new direction" (16–20); make them clearly different)

Between today and round 2 (dial retro back, keep depth):
- **11 Dämpad obsidian** — 07 toned down: today's rim geometry and nameplate, made of polished dark glass in the
  tint, gloss streak subtle; corner studs small and tinted (not red garnets everywhere); gem-inlay bars without
  the pixel segments; header gem dots kept.
- **12 Stilla glas** — 09 meets today's glass: a calm rim with the quiet bevel of today, only the corners carry a
  tiny 2-px step and the title tab gets small stepped shoulders; quiet cards; maximal clarity.
- **13 Milt fackelsken** — 10 without the heavy dither and torches as sprites: the pane as one block of material
  with a soft warm light from the top edge (tint-aware), carved hollows for cards/chips/bars, maybe a very fine
  dither only in the deepest shadows; the title carved, perhaps a tiny glowing ember mark instead of torches.
- **14 Glas på sten** — today's glass rim kept almost as is, but the content gets carved-stone depth (soft
  hollows, engraved text) and a fine grain shared with the rim so they read as one object.
- **15 Halvpixel** — modern smooth surfaces everywhere, pixel character only in a few accents (title-tab steps,
  bar end caps, header markers, stepped corners on chips). The "least retro" of the five.

New directions (still for this owner: depth, soft, slightly playful, Middle-earth, one object):
- **16 Emalj** — cloisonné: content cells of glossy enamel separated by thin raised metal wires in a metal tinted by
  the pane colour; frame as a metal bezel with the same wire. Jewellery-like, soft light.
- **17 Lykta** — the pane as a lantern: a thin dark metal frame, the content lit from within (soft inner glow in
  the tint, light falling off toward the edges); cards as panes of frosted glass. Should shine when transparent.
- **18 Läder och söm** — bound like a book: a leather frame with a stitched seam (fine dashed line) running round,
  title embossed/debossed; cards as softly embossed leather, bars as inset channels. Tactile, warm, tintable.
- **19 Mjukt djup** — no visible rim at all: the pane is a soft raised slab with a gentle large shadow and a soft
  inner well; the title a small debossed label. Modern soft depth; "border off" should feel almost identical.
- **20 Fri idé** — invent a new direction that fits the owner's taste and all constraints (e.g. elvish silver
  filigree line work, frost/rime on glass, map-table vellum with ink borders, woven runner…). Surprise us.
